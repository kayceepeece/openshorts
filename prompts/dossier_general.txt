# OpenShorts Prompt v1.0.0 — 2026-06-29
# File: dossier_general.txt

## Overview
- One-sentence summary of the video.
- Event type (e.g., podcast interview, sports broadcast, lecture, announcement, gameplay stream).
- Main subject or central topic.
- Source quality notes (audio clarity, camera work, lighting, crosstalk, editing style, any issues).

## Timeline
Break the video into logical [MM:SS–MM:SS] segments. For each segment, include:
- What is shown visually.
- What is said (key dialogue, not a full transcript).
- Who appears.
- What changes (mood, topic, action, camera, setting).
- Clip potential (low / medium / high) and one-sentence reason.

## Key Moments / Reveals
List every significant moment, reveal, announcement, or turning point with:
- Timestamp.
- What happens.
- Why it matters.
- Who is involved.
- Mark certainty: confirmed, inferred, or uncertain.

## Participants / People
### Confirmed names
For each: name, role, and how they are identified in the video.

### Inferred / unknown people
For each: physical description, clothing, position, role, and distinguishing features. Mark as inferred.

## Quotes
Include short verbatim quotes only when clearly audible and important. Include speaker and timestamp.

## Visual / Action Moments
For each notable visual action, reaction, demonstration, or performance:
- Timestamp.
- Subject / performer.
- Setup / context.
- Action type.
- Result / outcome.
- Crowd, audience, or co-panel reaction.
- Clip-worthy? (yes / no / maybe) and one-sentence reason.

## Best Clips
Rank the top moments for short-form clipping. For each:
- Timestamp range.
- Why it works as a clip.
- Suggested hook (first 2–3 seconds).
- Suggested payoff / ending.
- Standalone context score (1–3): does it make sense without the full video?

### How To Clip Compliance (evaluate EVERY candidate)
Each ranked clip must pass the three How To Clip rules. Record the result for every candidate.
  A) HOOK CHECK — the first 2–3 seconds must open with EITHER a bold statement OR a bold question.
     REJECT or re-cut anything opening with small talk, filler, pleasantries, or mid-thought context.
     If the strongest content has a weak open, use hook transplanting: pull a bold line made later in
     the video to the front (even repeated). The hook matters more than preserving the original order.
  B) POINT CHECK — the core value / reveal / payoff must arrive by the 10-second mark.
     Cut dead space, "um"/"uh", repetition, rambling, and thinking aloud ("let me think about how to say this").
     Compress so the point is clear within 10 seconds even if the clip itself runs longer.
  C) COMPLETE THOUGHT — the clip must end on a finished, rounded-out thought (satisfied, not cut off).
     REJECT clips that stop mid-point or end on an unresolved "but here's the thing…" with no resolution.
Reject any clip failing B or C. For a failed hook (A) with otherwise strong content, re-cut the start
(hook transplanting) instead of dropping the clip. Label each ranked clip with its compliance status,
e.g. "A✓ B✓ C✓ D3" or "A✗→transplant B✓ C✓ D2".

## Ambiguities
List anything unclear, partially hidden, cut off, or not confidently identifiable.

## Editor Notes
- Strongest hook in the video.
- Best cold-open start point.
- Likely short-form clips.
- Likely long-form recap segments.
- Any special editing considerations (jumps, B-roll, graphics, audio issues).
