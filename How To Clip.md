# How To Clip — Joshua's Framework for Clipping Social Content

*A practical guide on extracting valuable clips from long-form videos (podcasts, interviews, webinars, speeches, etc.).*

---

## The Core Problem

When you chop long-form content into short clips, you lose context. The challenge is maintaining **context, value, and attention** while compressing a 60-minute video into 60 seconds.

---

## The 3 Rules of a Good Clip

### 1. Start with a Hook — First 2-3 Seconds

The first thing out of someone's mouth must be either:

- **A bold statement**, or
- **A bold question**

That's it. If it's one of those two, it's probably a good hook. This is what captures the click-through rate.

**Pro tip (hook transplanting):** If a great piece of content has no good hook at the beginning, find a bold statement made later in the video and put it at the front too. Even if it's stated twice — who cares? You're starting with a strong hook, and that's what matters.

### 2. Get to the Point — First 10 Seconds

People give you 5–10 seconds to decide if they're staying. If the point isn't delivered within that window, they swipe away.

What to cut:
- Dead space — pauses, "um"s, "uh"s, filler words
- Repetition — things said more than once
- Rambling — stories that don't serve the point
- Thinking aloud — "let me think about how to say this"

After cutting the fluff, you compress the clip so the value arrives within the first 10 seconds. This doesn't mean the clip is over in 10 seconds — just that the viewer knows what they're getting by then.

### 3. End with a Complete Thought

The clip must feel finished. If it just cuts off mid-point or leaves a cliffhanger with no resolution, viewers won't want to watch from you again.

A complete thought means:
- The statement or argument was finished
- The point was rounded out
- The viewer feels satisfied, not cut off

---

## The Context Triangle

These 3 elements together deliver **context**:

```
        ┌──────────────┐
        │   Hook       │
        │ (foundation) │
        └──────┬───────┘
               │
        ┌──────▼───────┐
        │  Get to the  │
        │  point       │
        └──────┬───────┘
               │
        ┌──────▼───────┐
        │  Complete    │
        │  thought     │
        └──────────────┘
```

If you maintain these three things, the context will hold.

## Joshua's Practical Workflow

1. **Transcribe the video** — throw it into ChatGPT or similar
2. **Ask for timestamps** — "I need 10 clips from this 60-min podcast, where are bold questions and bold statements made?"
3. **Go clip at those timestamps** — extract the raw segments
4. **Check for context** — does it start well? Does it teach something? Does it end with a complete thought?
5. **Add CTAs** — you can record canned exits or overlay a call-to-action slide at the end

---

## How to Evaluate If a Clip Will Work

Watch a clip that went viral (100K+ views in under a day) and study it:

- Did it do something within the **first second**?
- Within the **first 5 seconds**?
- Within the **first 10 seconds**?
- All the way to the **60-second mark**?

If it went viral, it was because it had good context throughout — whether educational, informational, or entertainment.
