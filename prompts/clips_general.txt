## CLIP SELECTION FRAMEWORK — UNIVERSAL VIRALITY RULES
## Distilled from professional clip editor methodology (How To Clip)
## These rules apply to ALL content types. Domain-specific rules layer on top.

---

### RULE 1 — THE HOOK (Most Critical)

The first 2–3 seconds of a clip determine whether the viewer stays or swipes.
A strong hook is EXACTLY ONE of:
  A) A BOLD STATEMENT — a declarative claim that is surprising, counterintuitive, or strongly opinionated.
     Examples: "This is the most underrated skill in basketball." / "Nobody talks about this."
  B) A BOLD QUESTION — a question the viewer immediately wants answered.
     Examples: "What happens if you do this every day?" / "Did you know this is illegal?"

A clip FAILS the hook test if it starts with:
  - Small talk, pleasantries, or filler ("So, uh, today we're gonna...")
  - Context-setting that requires prior knowledge of the video
  - Mid-sentence that doesn't land as a standalone opening
  - A question or statement so generic it creates no curiosity

HOOK RESCUE TECHNIQUE: If the best content in a segment has a weak opening but a STRONG closing statement,
you MAY start the clip at a point slightly before that strong statement, even if it's mid-segment,
provided the opening still makes contextual sense as a standalone clip.
The goal: the strong statement should land within the first 5 seconds.

---

### RULE 2 — GET TO THE POINT IN < 10 SECONDS

After the hook, the viewer will give you 5–10 seconds to prove the clip is worth their time.
If the video hasn't delivered meaningful information, a revelation, or forward momentum by the 10-second mark,
the clip should be rejected or re-cut to a tighter start point.

This means:
  - Dead air, filler words ("um", "uh", "like", "you know"), and repetition are CUT POINTS.
    When you see these in the transcript, prefer starting the clip AFTER them.
  - If the speaker re-states the same idea twice before getting to the point,
    the clip should start at the second (clearer) statement, not the first.
  - Preamble like "So what I wanted to talk about today is..." is always a cut point.
    Start at the actual topic, not the announcement that it's coming.

---

### RULE 3 — STANDALONE CONTEXT

A clip must be fully understandable to someone who has NEVER seen the source video.
Clips that fail standalone context test:
  - Reference people or things without identifying them ("...and then HE said...")
  - Continue a train of thought started outside the clip window
  - Use pronouns without antecedents that appear in the clip
  - End on an unresolved "but here's the thing..." with the resolution outside the clip

When a clip has a context problem, widen the start point to include the necessary setup,
or narrow to a self-contained sub-segment. Never include a clip that will confuse a cold viewer.

---

### RULE 4 — THE COMPLETE THOUGHT ENDING

Every clip must end with a RESOLVED idea — a payoff, conclusion, or punctuation moment.
A clip FAILS the ending test if it:
  - Trails off mid-thought ("...and that's why I think you should really consider—")
  - Ends on a setup without the punchline
  - Cuts mid-sentence or mid-laugh
  - Leaves the viewer with an open question that won't be answered

Strong endings include:
  - A declarative conclusion to the argument made in the clip
  - A punchline or moment of clear emotional release
  - A resolved action (the dunk lands, the answer is given, the point is made)
  - A natural pause that signals the thought is complete

End the clip 0.2–0.5 seconds AFTER this resolution moment to let it breathe.

---

### RULE 5 — CLIP QUALITY RANKING CRITERIA

When ranking clips from best to worst, score each on these factors in order:

1. HOOK STRENGTH (0–3): Does the opening hook immediately create curiosity or attention?
   3 = bold statement/question that is genuinely surprising
   2 = decent hook but slightly generic
   1 = weak hook, requires fixing
   0 = no hook — clip starts with filler

2. STANDALONE VALUE (0–3): Does a cold viewer understand and enjoy this without context?
   3 = completely self-contained, adds value to anyone
   2 = mostly standalone, minor context needed
   1 = requires some prior knowledge
   0 = unintelligible without the surrounding video

3. PAYOFF CLARITY (0–3): Does the clip end satisfyingly?
   3 = clear resolution, strong ending
   2 = decent ending but slightly abrupt
   1 = trails off or cuts early
   0 = no payoff

4. RETENTION POTENTIAL (0–3): Would a viewer watch to the end?
   3 = pacing is tight, no dead air, consistently engaging
   2 = one or two slow moments but mostly tight
   1 = noticeable pacing issues
   0 = too slow or too long

Rank clips by TOTAL SCORE (max 12). Include only clips scoring ≥ 6.
If fewer than 3 clips score ≥ 6, lower the threshold to ≥ 4 rather than returning nothing.
