# OpenShorts Prompt v1.0.0 — 2026-06-29
# File: dossier_interview.txt

## Overview
- One-sentence summary of the interview.
- Format (one-on-one, panel interview, red carpet, documentary interview, press junket, podcast-style).
- Main subject or headline storyline.
- Source quality notes (audio clarity, camera angles, lighting, background, any technical issues).

## Timeline
Break the video into logical [MM:SS–MM:SS] segments. For each segment, include:
- What is shown visually (camera on interviewer, interviewee, B-roll, audience).
- What is said (key question or answer, not a full transcript).
- Who appears.
- What changes (topic, tone, emotional temperature, power dynamic).
- Clip potential (low / medium / high) and one-sentence reason.

## Interview Structure & Topics
List the major topics or phases of the interview with:
- Timestamp range.
- Topic.
- Who introduced it.
- Whether it was explored deeply or briefly.

## Key Questions Asked
For each significant question:
- Timestamp.
- Interviewer.
- The question (paraphrased or verbatim if short).
- Why the question matters or what it sets up.

## Notable Admissions & Revelations
For every confession, new detail, behind-the-scenes story, or surprising answer:
- Timestamp.
- Speaker.
- What was admitted or revealed.
- Why it matters.
- Certainty: confirmed or inferred.

## Tense, Emotional, or Dramatic Moments
For moments of pause, hesitation, visible emotion, pushback, deflection, or intensity:
- Timestamp.
- What happened.
- Who was involved.
- What changed in the conversation.
- Clip-worthy? (yes / no / maybe) and reason.

## Participants / Interviewers & Interviewees
### Confirmed names
For each: name, role (interviewer / interviewee / moderator), and how identified.

### Inferred / unknown people
For each: physical description, clothing, role, and distinguishing features. Mark as inferred.

## Best Quotes & Answers
Include short verbatim quotes only when clearly audible and important. Include speaker and timestamp.

## Best Clips
Rank the top moments for short-form clipping. For each:
- Timestamp range.
- Why it works as a clip (tension, revelation, emotional beat, clever answer, viral quote).
- Suggested hook (first 2–3 seconds).
- Suggested payoff / ending.
- Standalone context score (1–3): does it make sense without the full interview?

### How To Clip Compliance (evaluate EVERY candidate)
Each ranked clip must pass the three How To Clip rules. Record the result for every candidate.
  A) HOOK CHECK — the first 2–3 seconds must open with EITHER a bold statement OR a bold question.
     REJECT or re-cut anything opening with small talk, filler, pleasantries, or mid-thought context.
     If the strongest content has a weak open, use hook transplanting: pull a bold line made later in
     the video to the front (even repeated). The hook matters more than preserving the original order.
  B) POINT CHECK — the core reveal / answer / payoff must arrive by the 10-second mark.
     Cut dead space, "um"/"uh", repetition, rambling, and thinking aloud ("let me think about how to say this").
     Compress so the point is clear within 10 seconds even if the clip itself runs longer.
  C) COMPLETE THOUGHT — the clip must end on a finished, rounded-out thought (satisfied, not cut off).
     REJECT clips that stop mid-answer or end on an unresolved "but here's the thing…" with no resolution.
Reject any clip failing B or C. For a failed hook (A) with otherwise strong content, re-cut the start
(hook transplanting) instead of dropping the clip. Label each ranked clip with its compliance status,
e.g. "A✓ B✓ C✓ D3" or "A✗→transplant B✓ C✓ D2".

## Ambiguities
List anything unclear, partially hidden, cut off, or not confidently identifiable (names, facts, references, context).
