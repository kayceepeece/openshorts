## DRAMA / FILM CLIP SELECTION CRITERIA (Layered on top of Universal Rules)

In drama, film, and scripted content, clip selection should prioritize emotional clarity, scene integrity, and spoiler awareness.

### 1. HOOK SIGNAL (First 2–3s)
- **Character-driven hook**: A strong facial expression, physical action, or line of dialogue that immediately establishes mood or conflict.
- **Visual hook**: A striking shot, sudden movement, or dramatic lighting change that grabs attention without needing context.
- **Dialogue hook**: A bold statement, accusation, question, or confession that creates immediate curiosity.

Avoid starting a clip with:
- Establishing shots that contain no emotional information.
- Ambient small talk or transitional dialogue.
- Mid-scene entrances that require knowing previous scenes.

### 2. PAYOFF SIGNAL (The Climax & Resolution)
- **Emotional payoff**: The moment a character's reaction lands — a realization, a collapse, a triumph, a betrayal.
- **Narrative payoff**: A reveal, twist, decision, or resolution that completes the mini-arc within the clip.
- **Visual payoff**: The landing of an action beat, the reveal of a shot, or the punctuation of a comedic moment.

The clip must end on a moment that feels **emotionally complete**, not on a setup that needs the next scene to land.

### 3. SCENE INTEGRITY
- Prefer clips that respect natural scene or beat boundaries.
- If a scene has a weak opening but a strong ending, you may start slightly before the strong moment using the Hook Rescue Technique, provided the clip still makes sense as a standalone moment.
- Avoid cutting mid-sentence, mid-reaction, or mid-action unless the cut itself creates intentional impact.
- Preserve the emotional arc: tension → escalation → release. Do not cut the release out.

### 4. SPOILER AWARENESS
- Avoid clips that reveal major twists, deaths, betrayals, or ending-altering reveals unless the reveal itself is the hook and the clip is explicitly designed around it.
- Prefer moments that tease conflict or emotion without giving away the resolution.
- If a spoiler moment is genuinely the most viral part of the video, flag it with high spoiler risk but still include it if the hook justifies it.

### 5. PACING & SEGMENT BOUNDARIES
- Start the clip 1–2 seconds before the hook so the viewer has a split-second to orient.
- End the clip 0.5–1.5 seconds after the emotional payoff so it lands.
- Remove dead air, transitional shots, and filler dialogue inside the clip window.
- For action sequences, let the beat complete before cutting; for dialogue scenes, cut on the reaction that follows the line.
