# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Solo content creators running OpenShorts on their own machine between edit sessions. They're technical enough to self-host with Docker, comfortable with tools, and value speed and clarity over decoration. They're mid-task — uploading a video, monitoring a job, reviewing clips — and want the UI to disappear into the workflow.

## Product Purpose

OpenShorts is a self-hosted, open-source AI video platform that turns long-form videos into viral short clips, generates UGC-style marketing videos with AI actors, and provides a full YouTube Studio toolkit — all from a single local dashboard. It competes with Opus Clip, CapCut, and Vizard but wins on price (free), privacy (your server), and openness (no watermarks, no limits).

## Brand Personality

Fast · Sharp · Open — a pro tool for people who know what they're doing. Hacker-adjacent. No nonsense. The UI earns every pixel; nothing decorative, nothing soft.

## Anti-references

- Generic dark SaaS: blue/purple glassmorphism, gradient text headings, identical glass card grids — the current state, explicitly not the direction
- Vercel / Linear minimalist monochrome cool-gray — too quiet and too corporate for a video production tool
- Opus Clip / CapCut consumer-app pastels and neon — too glossy, too app-store
- Figma multicolor playful aesthetic — wrong register entirely

## Design Principles

1. **The tool disappears into the task.** Navigation, chrome, and status never compete with the user's content. Every surface exists to surface information or enable action, not to look impressive.
2. **Pro-grade density.** Information-dense by default — no empty whitespace for elegance's sake. When users are monitoring a job or reviewing 15 clips, they need everything visible.
3. **State-first design.** Every component earns its place by communicating state: idle, processing, done, error. The UI is most alive when a job is running, not when it's empty.
4. **Earned familiarity.** Reference: DaVinci Resolve — powerful, dark-native, no decoration. The interface should feel like a tool a professional would already trust without a learning curve.
5. **Open means nothing to hide.** Logs, progress, pipeline steps — surface them. The transparency of the open-source project should reflect in the UI: no spinning loaders hiding work; show the actual pipeline.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Readable without formal WCAG level requirement. Body text ≥4.5:1 contrast, interactive states clearly distinguishable, keyboard-navigable primary flows.
