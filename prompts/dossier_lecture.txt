## Best Clips
Rank the top moments for short-form clipping. For each:
- Timestamp range.
- Why it works as a clip (aha moment, counterintuitive insight, clear analogy, strong opener).
- Suggested hook (first 2–3 seconds).
- Suggested payoff / ending.
- Standalone context score (1–3): does it make sense without the full lecture?

### How To Clip Compliance (evaluate EVERY candidate)
Each ranked clip must pass the three How To Clip rules. Record the result for every candidate.
  A) HOOK CHECK — the first 2–3 seconds must open with EITHER a bold statement OR a bold question.
     REJECT or re-cut anything opening with small talk, filler, pleasantries, or mid-thought context.
     If the strongest content has a weak open, use hook transplanting: pull a bold line made later in
     the video to the front (even if repeated). The hook matters more than preserving the original order.
  B) POINT CHECK — the core takeaway must arrive by the 10-second mark.
     Cut dead space, "um"/"uh", repetition, rambling, and thinking aloud ("let me think about how to say this").
     Compress so the claim is clear within 10 seconds even if the clip itself runs longer.
  C) COMPLETE THOUGHT — the clip must finish on a completed takeaway (satisfied, not cut off).
     REJECT clips that stop mid-explanation or end with an unresolved "so the point is…" before the answer.
Reject any clip failing B or C. For a failed hook (A) with otherwise strong content, re-cut the start
(hook transplanting) instead of dropping the clip. Label each ranked clip with its compliance status,
e.g. "A✓ B✓ C✓ D3" or "A✗→transplant B✓ C✓ D2".
